Closest Beach to San Antonio, Texas

Drive routes, water quality, and how the Gulf Coast options compare

Published on November 2, 2026

If you live in San Antonio and want to spend a day or a weekend on the beach, the Texas Gulf Coast is closer than you might think. Several beach towns sit within a few hours' drive, and each one offers a different experience. Here is how the main options compare and why Port Aransas tends to be the top pick for San Antonio travelers.

The Drive from San Antonio to Port Aransas

The most popular route takes I-37 south to Corpus Christi, then TX-358 east across the JFK Causeway to North Padre Island, and TX-361 north onto Mustang Island. This route skips the ferry entirely and drops you right into the Cinnamon Shore area and Port Aransas.

Traffic on I-37 is generally lighter than the I-35 corridor that Austin travelers deal with, and the drive is mostly flat, open highway. For the most current drive times and road conditions, check a maps app before you head out, but most San Antonio residents are looking at roughly three and a half to four hours depending on traffic and stops.

If you are heading down on a Friday, leaving before 2 PM or after 7 PM helps you avoid the worst of the weekend rush heading south. On Sunday, try to leave the island before early afternoon to stay ahead of return traffic.

Port Aransas and Mustang Island

Port Aransas is the destination most San Antonio families and groups end up choosing, and for good reason. The town sits on Mustang Island with miles of wide, drive-on Gulf beach, cleaner water than the upper coast, and a laid-back small-town atmosphere that makes it easy to unwind.

The town itself is walkable and full of things to do beyond the beach. Fishing charters run daily out of the harbor, and the jetties and Horace Caldwell Pier are popular spots for shore anglers. Horseback riding along the beach is one of the most popular activities for first-time visitors. Golf cart rentals are the preferred way to cruise around town. Dolphin watching tours, paddleboarding, and kayaking are all easy to book.

The free ferry from Aransas Pass to Port Aransas runs 24 hours a day and is an experience in itself, with dolphins often spotted alongside the boats. That said, you can skip the ferry entirely by taking the JFK Causeway route from Corpus Christi.

Rockport and Fulton

Rockport is a charming bay town that sits on Aransas Bay, a bit closer to San Antonio than Port Aransas. It is known for fishing, birding, art galleries, and a relaxed waterfront atmosphere. The Aransas National Wildlife Refuge is nearby and is one of the best places in the country to see whooping cranes during winter months.

The catch with Rockport is that it does not have a Gulf surf beach. The waterfront is bay-side, so if you are looking for waves and open sand, you will need to continue south to Mustang Island or Port Aransas. Rockport works well as a day trip or a stop along the way, especially for birders and anglers.

Corpus Christi

Corpus Christi is the largest city on the Texas Gulf Coast and sits right on the route to Port Aransas. It has its own beaches on North Padre Island (accessed via the JFK Causeway), plus attractions like the USS Lexington, the Texas State Aquarium, and a solid restaurant scene along the bayfront.

For a quick overnight trip, Corpus can work as a base, but most travelers who want a true beach vacation prefer to keep driving the extra 30 to 45 minutes to Port Aransas, where the beaches are less urban and the vibe is more relaxed.

South Padre Island

South Padre Island has the clearest water on the Texas coast and beautiful beaches. It is significantly farther from San Antonio than Port Aransas, adding at least an extra hour or more to the drive. South Padre also draws heavy spring break crowds in March.

If you have a full week and want the best water clarity Texas can offer, South Padre is worth the longer drive. For a weekend getaway from San Antonio, Port Aransas is the more practical choice.

Galveston

Galveston is the biggest beach town in Texas and offers the most in terms of restaurants, nightlife, and attractions like the Strand District, Moody Gardens, and Pleasure Pier. However, it sits on the upper Texas coast where the water tends to be brown and murky from bay sediment. It is also farther from San Antonio than Port Aransas and sits in the opposite direction, making it a less convenient option.

For San Antonio residents, Galveston rarely makes sense as a beach destination when Port Aransas is closer and has cleaner water.

Weekend Trip Tips

A three-night stay is the sweet spot for a San Antonio beach trip. Arrive Thursday evening or Friday morning, enjoy two or three full beach days, and head home Sunday afternoon. This gives you enough time to actually relax without burning half your trip on the road.

Dolphin watching tours are a great way to spend a morning, especially with kids. The things to do in Port Aransas go well beyond the beach, so even if the weather is not perfect, you will not run out of options.

Tip: If you want to explore the wilder side of the island, Mustang Island State Park is about 10 minutes south of Cinnamon Shore. It has five miles of undeveloped beach, camping, a paddling trail on the bay side, and some of the best stargazing on the coast.
