Planning a Family Reunion at Cinnamon Shore

How to bring 20 to 40 people together on Mustang Island without losing your mind

Published on November 6, 2026

A beach reunion sounds great in theory. In practice, it falls apart when half the family is in a hotel across town, nobody can agree on dinner, and the logistics turn whoever organized it into a full-time project manager. Cinnamon Shore in Port Aransas solves most of that by putting everything within walking distance: the beach, pools, restaurants, and enough rental homes to keep the whole family on the same block.

Here is how to make it work for groups of 20 to 40 people without turning it into a second job.

The Lodging Strategy

The key to a good family reunion is keeping everyone close but giving each household its own space. At Cinnamon Shore, you can book two to four homes on the same street or within a short walk of each other. Each home has its own bedrooms, kitchen, and porch, so families with young kids can keep their own schedule while still being two minutes from the main gathering spot.

Pick one home as the anchor house. This is where group meals happen, where everyone meets in the morning for coffee, and where the cousins end up playing games after dinner. The anchor home should have the biggest common area, a solid kitchen, and ideally an elevator for grandparents or anyone with mobility needs.

The other homes are for sleeping. People head back to their own house when they need quiet, nap time, or a break from the group. It sounds simple, but this setup is what separates a reunion that works from one where everyone is stepping over each other by day two.

How Cinnamon Shore Compares

If you are weighing options, here is how a Cinnamon Shore reunion stacks up against the alternatives.

Galveston beach houses give you the Gulf Coast experience, but the water is brown, the beaches are more crowded, and finding multiple homes on the same block is harder. The town is also more spread out, which means more driving between activities.

Texas Hill Country resorts are a solid option if your family does not care about the beach. The scenery is beautiful, but you lose the sand-and-surf element that makes a reunion feel like a real vacation for kids and adults alike.

Florida mega-homes (30A or Destin) have gorgeous water and big rental houses, but the drive or flight from Texas adds a full travel day each way. For a three or four-night reunion, that is a lot of transit time. Port Aransas is roughly three hours from San Antonio and under four from Austin or Houston, which means more time at the beach and less on the road.

Setting the Daily Rhythm

The best reunions do not have an hour-by-hour schedule. They have a loose rhythm that gives people anchor points without making anyone feel herded.

A typical day might look like this: pool and coffee in the morning, beach around mid-morning, lunch at the house or a walk to the village, free time in the afternoon, group dinner in the evening, and a fire pit or stargazing session after dark. Post a simple one-page itinerary with addresses, meal times, and any activities that need a headcount. Share it in the group text and let people opt in and out as they want.

The key is having one or two scheduled group events each day and leaving the rest open. Trying to keep 30 people on the same minute-by-minute schedule will make everyone miserable, including you.

Group Activities That Actually Work

Beach and pools. This is the default activity and honestly the best one. Cinnamon Shore has community pools and direct beach access, so the group can split between sand and water depending on age and preference. Set up a canopy and chairs in the morning and let people rotate in and out all day.

Dolphin watching. A dolphin watching boat tour is a hit with every age group. For larger reunions, you can book a private charter that fits 12 or more people. The tours run about two hours and dolphins show up on nearly every trip.

Golf cart cruising. Rent at least one golf cart per house. Kids love them, grandparents appreciate not walking everywhere, and the whole group can caravan into town for ice cream or sunset walks along the shore. Most rental companies deliver to the property.

Horseback riding. Beach horseback rides on Mustang Island are one of the most popular activities in Port Aransas. It is a memorable experience for older kids and adults, and the photos are hard to beat.

Mustang Island State Park. A morning trip to Mustang Island State Park gives the group five miles of undeveloped beach, a paddling trail on the bay side, and some of the best stargazing on the coast. It is about ten minutes south of Cinnamon Shore.

Rainy day backup. If the weather turns, Corpus Christi is 30 to 45 minutes away. The USS Lexington (a World War II aircraft carrier you can walk through) and the Texas State Aquarium are both great for families and can fill a full afternoon.

Meal Planning for Large Groups

Trying to take 30 people to a restaurant for every meal is a recipe for frustration. The approach that works best is a mix: cook at the anchor house most nights, eat out once or twice, and bring in a private chef for one special dinner.

Cooking at the house. Assign each household a night to cook, or pool money for groceries and split the cooking. The anchor home's kitchen handles this well, and the porch and great room give you enough space for a big group dinner. Stop at H-E-B in Portland or Corpus Christi on the way in for a full grocery run.

Restaurant dinners. For the nights you eat out, book early and call the restaurant directly. Online booking systems do not always show availability for groups of 15 or more. The Port Aransas dining scene is casual and group-friendly, but the popular spots fill up on weekends.

Private chef. This is the move for the one big reunion dinner. A chef comes to the house, cooks a multi-course meal, and handles cleanup. Book at least two weeks ahead during peak season. The Cinnamon Shore concierge can help arrange this.

Booking Timeline

Family reunions require more lead time than a regular trip because you are coordinating multiple homes and group activities. Here is a rough timeline.

6 to 9 months ahead: Book the rental homes. Summer, holiday weekends, and spring break fill up early, especially for groups that need multiple homes on the same street. The earlier you lock in the houses, the more options you have.

4 to 6 weeks ahead: Reserve group restaurant dinners and book the private chef. Also book any group activities that require advance reservations, like a private dolphin charter or horseback riding for a large party.

4 weeks ahead: Book a group photographer if you want professional family photos on the beach. This is optional but worth it when you have the whole family together.

2 weeks ahead: Send the group itinerary with addresses, meal assignments, activity times, and any packing reminders. Share grocery lists so whoever arrives first can do the big shop.

Tip: If you are booking multiple nonrefundable rentals, consider group travel insurance. One cancelled flight or a family emergency can leave you holding the bill for a house nobody used.

Best Time to Go

Late May through June and mid-August through September are the best windows for a family reunion. The weather is warm, the water is swimmable, and the crowds are thinner than peak summer. You will also find better rental rates and easier restaurant reservations. For more detail on seasonal conditions, check the best time to visit Port Aransas guide.

Avoid March if you want to dodge spring break traffic, and keep in mind that July is the hottest and busiest month on the island.

Getting There

Corpus Christi International Airport (CRP) is the closest commercial airport, about 35 to 45 minutes from Cinnamon Shore. San Antonio (SAT) and Austin (AUS) are alternatives with more flight options if your family is flying in from different cities.

For families driving in, the JFK Causeway route through Corpus Christi is the most straightforward. Take I-37 south to Corpus, TX-358 across the causeway to North Padre Island, then TX-361 north onto Mustang Island. No ferry, no wait.
