Bay, jetty, pier, surf, offshore, and fly fishing on the Texas Gulf Coast

Port Aransas has been a fishing town since long before it became a vacation destination. The marina has run charters since the 1930s, and the Deep Sea Roundup tournament has been held every summer since 1932. Whether you are a first-timer looking to rent a rod at the pier or an experienced angler chasing bull reds on the jetties, there are six distinct ways to fish within five miles of town. This guide covers all of them, along with the charters, outfitters, tournaments, and species you should know about before your trip.

Tip: Check the Port Aransas water color forecast before heading out. Clearer water usually means better sight-fishing and jetty days, while stained water can push fish into different patterns.

Six Ways to Fish Port Aransas

What makes Port Aransas stand out from other Texas coastal towns is the variety. You can fish from the beach in the morning, hit the pier after lunch, and book an offshore charter for the next day. Here is how each option works and who it is best for.

Bay Fishing

Bay fishing is the most beginner-friendly option in Port Aransas and one of the most productive. The Lighthouse Lakes area and the surrounding bays hold healthy populations of redfish, speckled trout, and flounder throughout most of the year. A guided bay trip is the easiest way to get started, since your captain will know exactly where the fish are biting and provide all the gear. Kayak anglers also do well in the shallow flats, especially on calm mornings when you can sight-cast to tailing reds.

Jetty Fishing

The granite jetties at Aransas Pass are among the most iconic fishing spots on the Texas coast. This is intermediate-level fishing that rewards patience and preparation. Redfish, jack crevalle, sharks, and speckled trout are all regular catches from the rocks. The jetties also produce tarpon during the late summer months. Wear shoes with good grip, watch the swell before you commit to a spot, and bring a long-handled net for landing fish from the rocks.

Pier Fishing

Horace Caldwell Pier is the go-to spot for anyone who wants to fish without a boat. The pier extends into the Gulf and is open to the public for a small gate fee. Whiting, croaker, redfish, and sharks are common catches year-round, and king mackerel show up on the pier during summer. You can rent rods and buy bait at the pier shop, which makes this one of the lowest-barrier entry points for fishing in Port Aransas.

Surf Fishing

Casting from the beach is one of the simplest and most rewarding ways to fish here. The stretch of sand at Cinnamon Shore is about a two-minute walk from Beached Inn, which makes early morning surf sessions incredibly convenient. Target the first and second sandbars for whiting, pompano, redfish, and speckled trout. Fall is the best season for surf fishing on Mustang Island, when migrating fish move through the area in large numbers.

Offshore and Deep-Sea Fishing

The offshore fleet operates out of the Port Aransas marina near the ferry landing. Half-day and full-day charters target king mackerel, red snapper (during federal season), mahi-mahi, amberjack, and tuna farther out in the Gulf. Most charter companies supply all the gear, bait, tackle, and fish-cleaning services. This is a charter-only option, and booking in advance is recommended during peak summer months.

Fly Fishing

Fly fishing in Port Aransas is centered on the Lighthouse Lakes flats, where anglers sight-cast to redfish cruising the shallows. This is intermediate to advanced fishing that requires patience and accurate casting. If you are new to saltwater fly fishing, hiring a guide is strongly recommended. The best months are spring and fall when redfish are most active on the flats and the wind cooperates more often.

Charters and Outfitters

Port Aransas has a deep roster of fishing charters and outfitters that have been operating for decades. Here are three of the most established operations in town.

Fisherman's Wharf offers bay fishing trips, deep-sea charters, and dolphin-watching tours. They also have a bait and tackle shop on site, making it a convenient one-stop before heading out on the water.

Deep Sea Headquarters specializes in kingfish, snapper, and group Gulf charters. They run a variety of trip lengths and are one of the longest-running charter operations at the marina.

Woody's Sports Center is a local institution for bait, tackle, fishing licenses, and charter bookings. If you need to grab live shrimp or pick up a last-minute rod, this is where locals and visitors go.

Fishing Without a Charter

You do not need to book a charter to have a great day of fishing in Port Aransas. There are several public-access options that require nothing more than a rod, some bait, and a fishing license.

Horace Caldwell Pier is open day and night with a small gate fee. The pier shop rents rods and sells bait, so you can show up empty-handed and still fish. It is one of the best family-friendly fishing options in town.

The jetties on both the Mustang Island and San Jose Island sides offer free access. The San Jose side requires a short passenger ferry ride from Fisherman's Wharf, which adds a small adventure to the trip.

The beach at Cinnamon Shore is open for surf fishing and is just a short walk from Beached Inn. Set up your rod holders in the sand, cast into the surf, and wait for a bite.

Roberts Point Park has a lighted public pier along the ferry channel. It is a relaxed spot that works well for families and kids, especially in the evening when the lights attract baitfish and the predators that follow them.

Fishing Tournaments

Port Aransas hosts some of the most well-known fishing tournaments on the Texas coast, drawing anglers from across the state and beyond.

The Deep Sea Roundup takes place every July and has been running since 1932, making it one of the oldest fishing tournaments in Texas. It includes bay, offshore, and youth divisions, with an awards ceremony and community events throughout the weekend. If your trip overlaps with the Roundup, it is worth watching the weigh-ins at the marina even if you are not competing. Check the Port Aransas events calendar for exact dates.

Babes on the Bay is a women-only inshore tournament held in the spring in the Rockport and Aransas Bay area. It raises money for local charities and has grown into one of the largest women's fishing events in the country.

What Bites When: A Seasonal Species Guide

Knowing what fish are running during your visit helps you choose the right trip and set realistic expectations. Here is a breakdown of the most popular species and their peak seasons. Always check current Texas Parks and Wildlife Department regulations for bag limits and size restrictions before keeping any fish.

Redfish are available year-round and are the bread-and-butter species for Port Aransas anglers. Slot reds are caught in the bays and surf throughout the year, while the big bull reds show up along the jetties and beachfront from September through November.

Speckled trout fishing is best from March through June and again from September through November. The spring run brings large spawning trout into the bays, and the fall produces consistent numbers in the shallow flats.

Flounder are an inshore favorite, and the October through November migration is one of the highlights of the fishing calendar. During the fall run, flounder stack up in the passes and channels as they head toward the Gulf to spawn.

Tarpon move through the Port Aransas jetties from August through October. These are powerful fish that put on an incredible fight, and they are catch-and-release only in Texas waters.

King mackerel are available from May through October. They are caught offshore on charters and occasionally from Horace Caldwell Pier during the summer months, which makes for some of the most exciting pier fishing you will find anywhere.

Red snapper season in the federal Gulf waters typically opens in early summer. The exact dates change each year, so check TPWD and NOAA announcements before booking an offshore trip specifically for snapper.

Mahi-mahi and tuna are offshore species available from May through September. You will need a deep-sea charter to reach them, and the best action tends to be 40 or more miles out in the Gulf.

Tips for First-Time Anglers

If you have never fished in saltwater before, Port Aransas is one of the best places to start. Here are a few things to keep in mind before your first trip.

A Texas saltwater fishing license with a saltwater endorsement is required for anyone 17 and older. You can buy one online through the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department website, at local tackle shops like Woody's Sports Center, or at most sporting goods stores. Kids under 17 fish for free.

For your first outing, the two easiest options are booking a half-day bay charter or heading to Horace Caldwell Pier. A bay charter takes the guesswork out of the experience since your guide handles everything. The pier is the most budget-friendly option and lets you fish at your own pace with rental gear available on site.

Tip: Charters supply all gear, bait, and tackle. If you are fishing on your own, live shrimp is the most versatile bait and works for almost every inshore species. Pick some up at any of the local tackle shops or the bait stand at the marina.

No matter how you choose to fish, Port Aransas delivers. The variety of options, the accessibility for beginners, and the quality of the fishery make it one of the top saltwater destinations in Texas. Plan your trip around the species you want to target, bring sunscreen and plenty of water, and enjoy your time on the water.
