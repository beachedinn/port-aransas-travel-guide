Port Aransas FAQ: Wildlife, Safety & the Water

Everything families need to know before a beach trip to Mustang Island

Planning a trip to Port Aransas and not sure what to expect? This guide covers the questions visitors ask most often, from what animals you might spot on Mustang Island to whether the water is safe for swimming. Port Aransas is generally a safe and welcoming beach town, and a little preparation goes a long way toward a worry-free vacation.

What animals live on Mustang Island?

Mustang Island is home to bottlenose dolphins, brown pelicans, herons, and ghost crabs. Kemp's ridley sea turtles nest on the dunes in spring and summer. Birders can spot roseate spoonbills, ospreys, and, during winter months, whooping cranes at the Aransas National Wildlife Refuge. The waters hold redfish, speckled trout, flounder, and small coastal sharks.

Are there alligators in Port Aransas?

Alligators are very uncommon on Mustang Island. The nearest reliable populations are found inland near the Aransas refuge. Visitors spending time on the beach, boardwalks, and around town should not expect to encounter them.

What about rattlesnakes?

Western diamondbacks do live in dune grasses on the island. The best approach is to stay on marked crossovers and boardwalks, keep dogs on a leash, and avoid reaching into vegetation you cannot see through. The open beach and the Cinnamon Shore walkways are not typical habitat for rattlesnakes.

Are there sharks in Port Aransas?

Blacktip, bonnethead, and sandbar sharks are present in Gulf waters, but encounters with swimmers are rare. To stay safe, swim during daylight hours, avoid swimming alone, stay away from anglers using bait, skip shiny jewelry in the water, and avoid swimming when the water is murky. For a deeper look, check our full guide to sharks in Port Aransas.

Is it safe to swim in Port Aransas?

Generally, yes. Rip currents are the main risk for swimmers. Always check the daily beach flag before getting in the water, swim near other people or a lifeguard, and if you get caught in a rip current, swim parallel to shore until you are free, then head back in. Port Aransas is a popular family beach destination, and most visitors enjoy the water without any issues.

Tip: Check the beach flag colors each morning before heading out. Green means calm conditions. Yellow means use caution. Red means strong currents or high surf. Double red means the beach is closed to swimming. Purple means dangerous marine life has been spotted.

Are there jellyfish?

Moon jellies and cabbage-head jellies are common but mostly harmless. The real concern is Portuguese man o' war, which sometimes wash ashore with southeast winds during spring and fall. Watch for blue ribbons on the sand and avoid them. If stung, leave the water, rinse the area with seawater (not fresh water), remove tentacles with a hard edge like a credit card, and seek medical attention if pain or swelling gets worse.

Where is the nearest hospital or ER?

There is no hospital on Mustang Island. Port Aransas EMS operates two ambulances around the clock, and you should always dial 911 for emergencies. The closest medical facilities include Surepoint Emergency Center on Padre Island (a 24/7 ER, about 15 minutes away), Access Total Care urgent care on Padre Island, Corpus Christi Medical Center Bay Area (about 25 minutes north), and CHRISTUS Spohn Shoreline, the regional trauma and stroke center in downtown Corpus Christi.

Can you surf in Port Aransas?

Yes. Typical waves run between 1 and 3 feet, with bigger swells arriving from fall and winter cold fronts and summer tropical systems. Popular spots include the area around Horace Caldwell Pier and the main beach. Local shops rent surfboards and wetsuits and offer lessons for beginners. If you are looking for things to do in Port Aransas, surfing is a great option even for first-timers.

What about sea turtles?

Kemp's ridley sea turtles nest along the beach from April through July. If you spot a nesting or stranded turtle, do not approach or touch it. Call the Padre Island National Seashore hotline at 1-866-TURTLE-5 (1-866-887-8535). Hatchling releases are sometimes open to the public. Check the National Park Service for announcements during your visit.

Can you see dolphins?

Bottlenose dolphins are common year-round in Aransas Bay and the nearshore waters. You can often see them from the beach, the jetties, and the ferry channel without a boat. Local operators also run dolphin-watching tours from the harbor if you want a closer look.

Are mosquitoes a problem?

Mosquitoes are most active at dawn, dusk, and after summer rain. Use a repellent with DEET or picaridin and consider wearing long sleeves around sunset. The beach itself is usually breezy enough to keep them away. They tend to be more common in shaded yards and near the bay side of the island.

When is hurricane season?

Hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, with the highest risk from August through October. Most weeks during this window are completely uneventful, but it is smart to check National Hurricane Center forecasts before traveling during peak months. The Port Aransas beach conditions page is also a good resource for current weather updates.
