Padre Island National Seashore

70 miles of undeveloped Gulf Coast barrier island, just south of Port Aransas

Home

Published on November 24, 2026

Padre Island National Seashore is one of the longest stretches of undeveloped barrier island in the world, covering roughly 70 miles of open Gulf beach along the Texas coast. Managed by the National Park Service, it offers a completely different experience from the developed beach towns nearby. There are no high-rises, no boardwalks, and very few people once you get past the visitor center area.

For visitors staying in Port Aransas, the national seashore makes an excellent day trip. The drive from Cinnamon Shore takes about 45 minutes, and you can easily spend a full day exploring the beach, watching wildlife, or just enjoying the solitude. Here is everything you need to know before you go.

How to Get There from Port Aransas

From Port Aransas, head south on TX-361 along Mustang Island. Cross the JFK Causeway into Corpus Christi, then follow the signs south on Park Road 22 to the national seashore entrance. The drive is straightforward and scenic.

Tip: There is no fuel inside the park. Fill up your tank before crossing the causeway. The last reliable gas stations are in Corpus Christi along the route. For detailed route options from major Texas cities, see the directions to Port Aransas guide.

Entrance Fees

Padre Island National Seashore charges an entrance fee at the gate.

7-Day Pass: $25 per private vehicle or motorcycle. This covers everyone in the vehicle for a full week.

1-Day Pass: $10 per vehicle.

Annual and Interagency Passes: The America the Beautiful interagency pass is accepted and covers entrance to all national parks and federal recreation areas for a full year.

Things to Do at Padre Island National Seashore

Malaquite Beach and Visitor Center

Malaquite Beach is the main developed area and the best starting point for first-time visitors. It has paved access, a large parking lot, restrooms, outdoor showers, and a covered pavilion. The visitor center includes exhibits on the island's ecology and a small store with snacks, ice, and basic supplies. There is no full food service inside the park, so bring lunch and plenty of water.

The beach here is wide, clean, and far less crowded than the public beaches in Port Aransas or Corpus Christi. It is a great spot for families, with easy access and lifeguard coverage during peak season.

Beach Driving

One of the unique features of Padre Island National Seashore is that you can drive on the beach for roughly 60 miles south of the visitor center. The first five miles past Malaquite are accessible to most vehicles in dry conditions, though soft sand is always a possibility.

Past Mile 5, a 4WD vehicle with high clearance is strongly recommended. Past Mile 20, experienced off-road driving skills and proper preparation are essential. The farther south you go, the more remote and isolated the beach becomes.

Beach Driving Tips: Lower your tire pressure to around 20 psi before hitting soft sand. Carry a tow strap, since tow assistance inside the park is scarce and expensive. Cell phone coverage fades past Mile 30 and disappears entirely further south. Always let someone know your plans before heading deep into the park.

Bird Island Basin

Bird Island Basin is on the bay side of the island and offers a completely different experience from the Gulf beach. The shallow, calm waters of the Laguna Madre make it one of the best spots on the Texas coast for windsurfing, kiteboarding, and stand-up paddleboarding. Kayaking and wade fishing are also popular here.

Access is through a separate road inside the park. The basin has its own parking area, primitive camping, and boat launch. If you enjoy water activities, this is worth a stop.

Big Shell Beach

About 30 miles south of the visitor center, Big Shell Beach is known for large, intact seashells that wash ashore along this remote stretch. Getting there requires a 4WD vehicle, solid off-road experience, and plenty of preparation. The reward is a truly wild, empty beach with some of the best shelling on the Texas coast.

Sea Turtles at Padre Island National Seashore

Padre Island National Seashore is one of the most important nesting sites in the world for the Kemp's ridley sea turtle, the most endangered sea turtle species. The park runs an active monitoring and protection program during nesting season.

Public hatchling releases typically take place on Malaquite Beach from mid-June through August. Releases happen at first light and are announced on short notice, so timing a visit around one requires some flexibility. The park maintains a hatchling release hotline at 361-949-7163 for schedule updates.

Camping

The national seashore offers two types of camping.

Malaquite Campground: A developed campground near the visitor center with designated sites, some with hookups. This is the most accessible option and works well for RVs and families who want restrooms and showers nearby.

Primitive Beach Camping: You can camp on the open beach in designated zones south of the developed area. This is true primitive camping with no facilities, no shade, and no services. Bring everything you need, including water, shade structures, and a way to pack out all trash.

Wildlife and Birding

Beyond sea turtles, the national seashore supports a diverse ecosystem. The island sits along the Central Flyway, making it a significant stop for migratory birds in spring and fall. Shorebirds, raptors, songbirds, and waterfowl all pass through in large numbers.

The Laguna Madre side of the island is especially productive for birding, with wading birds, pelicans, terns, and skimmers common year-round. For serious birders, a visit to the nearby Aransas National Wildlife Refuge pairs well with a day at the seashore.

Other wildlife you may encounter includes coyotes, jackrabbits, ghost crabs, and occasionally dolphins visible from the beach. For information on other marine life in the area, see the guides on sharks, jellyfish, and alligators near Port Aransas.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (March through May): Mild temperatures, good birding during migration, and manageable crowds. One of the best windows for a visit.

Summer (June through August): Hot and sunny with the chance to see sea turtle hatchling releases. Bring extra water, sun protection, and plan for intense afternoon heat.

Fall (September through November): Warm water, fewer visitors, and excellent fall bird migration. Another great time to visit.

Winter (December through February): Quieter and cooler. Whooping crane viewing is available nearby at the Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, though not within the national seashore itself.

Practical Tips

Dogs: Dogs are allowed on a leash of six feet or less. Some areas are restricted during sea turtle nesting season to protect nests and hatchlings.

Water and food: The visitor center has a small store with snacks and ice, but no restaurant or food service. Bring a cooler with lunch, snacks, and more water than you think you will need.

Sun protection: There is almost no natural shade on the island. Bring sunscreen, hats, and a shade structure if you plan to spend a full day.

Fishing: Surf fishing is allowed along the entire beach with a valid Texas fishing license. The national seashore offers excellent surf fishing opportunities for redfish, whiting, and pompano.

Padre Island National Seashore vs South Padre Island

These are two completely different places despite sharing a name. Padre Island National Seashore is an undeveloped, federally protected barrier island with no hotels, restaurants, or commercial development. South Padre Island is a developed resort town at the southern tip of Texas with high-rise condos, nightlife, and tourist infrastructure.

There is no road connecting the two. You cannot drive from the national seashore to South Padre Island. For a detailed comparison of Port Aransas and South Padre, see the Port Aransas vs South Padre Island guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Padre Island National Seashore from Port Aransas?
About 45 minutes by car. Head south on TX-361 across the JFK Causeway, then follow Park Road 22 to the entrance.

Do I need a 4WD vehicle?
Not for Malaquite Beach and the first few miles of beach driving. Past Mile 5, 4WD with high clearance is strongly recommended. Past Mile 20, it is essential.

Can I see sea turtle hatchling releases?
Yes, from mid-June through August at Malaquite Beach. Releases happen at dawn on short notice. Call the hotline at 361-949-7163 for updates.

Is there food inside the park?
Only a small store at the visitor center with snacks and ice. Bring your own food and water.

Can I camp on the beach?
Yes. Primitive beach camping is allowed in designated zones. The Malaquite Campground near the visitor center offers developed sites with some hookups.

Is Padre Island National Seashore the same as South Padre Island?
No. They are completely separate locations with no road connection between them. The national seashore is undeveloped parkland; South Padre is a resort town.

Are dogs allowed?
Yes, on a leash of six feet or less. Some areas are restricted during nesting season.
