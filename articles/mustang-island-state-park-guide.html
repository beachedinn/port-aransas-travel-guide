Mustang Island State Park: A Visitor's Guide

Camping, fishing, paddling, and five miles of undeveloped Gulf beach

Home

Published on October 31, 2026

About 10 minutes south of Cinnamon Shore on TX-361, Mustang Island State Park is one of the best stretches of natural coastline left on the Texas Gulf Coast. The park covers roughly 3,950 acres, including around five miles of undeveloped beach, rolling dunes, and a section of Corpus Christi Bay on the back side. There are no condos, no boardwalks, and no souvenir shops. It is just sand, water, wildlife, and sky.

Whether you are looking for a quiet morning of surf fishing, a multi-day camping trip, or just a few hours on a beach that feels a little wilder than the main stretch in town, Mustang Island State Park is worth the short drive from Port Aransas.

What Makes the Park Different from the Town Beach

Port Aransas has a long public beach that is easy to access, great for families, and close to restaurants and shops. Mustang Island State Park offers a different kind of experience. The beach here is undeveloped, meaning you will not see rows of umbrellas, golf carts rolling past, or vendors set up on the sand. It is quieter, less crowded, and feels more removed from town life.

The park gates stay open 24 hours a day, which means you can catch a sunrise over the Gulf without competing for space, or stay late for some of the best stargazing on the island. Light pollution is minimal compared to town, and on a clear night the Milky Way is visible from the beach.

Surf Fishing

Fishing is one of the main draws at Mustang Island State Park. The beach is open to surf fishing along its entire length, and the waters here hold redfish, speckled trout, black drum, whiting, and the occasional small shark. Wade fishing in the first and second sandbars is popular, especially early in the morning and around sunset.

You will need a valid Texas saltwater fishing license, which you can purchase online through the Texas Parks and Wildlife website or at most bait shops in Port Aransas. Live bait like shrimp and mullet works well, and cut bait is effective for larger species. The Paddling Trail

On the bay side of the park, a 20-mile paddling trail winds through shallow flats and coastal marsh. The trail is divided into five color-coded routes that vary in length and difficulty, so it works for both beginners and experienced paddlers. The water is calm compared to the Gulf side, and you are likely to see herons, egrets, roseate spoonbills, and possibly dolphins.

Kayak and paddleboard rentals are available from outfitters in Port Aransas. If you plan to bring your own, there is a launch point inside the park on the bay side. Early mornings are the best time to paddle, when the wind is lightest and the wildlife is most active.

Camping

The park has two types of camping:

Drive-up sites are located inland from the beach and come with water and electric hookups. These are the most popular spots and fill up fast, especially during spring break, summer weekends, and holiday periods. If you are planning a camping trip during peak season, book through the Texas State Parks reservation system as far in advance as possible. Sites can fill months ahead.

Primitive walk-in sites are closer to the dunes and offer a more stripped-down experience. There are no hookups, and the restroom and shower facilities are a walk away. What you get in return is a campsite that feels much closer to the beach, with nothing between you and the sound of the waves.

Birding and Wildlife

Mustang Island State Park is part of the Great Texas Coastal Birding Trail, and birders come from across the country to walk the dune flats and back-island marsh. Common species include brown pelicans, great blue herons, reddish egrets, black skimmers, and ospreys. During fall and spring migration, the variety increases significantly.

Beyond birds, you may spot ghost crabs on the beach at night, coyotes in the dunes at dawn, and the occasional sea turtle nest during nesting season (April through July). The park asks visitors to respect wildlife by keeping their distance and not disturbing nesting areas.

Beach Driving

The park allows driving on a marked strip of beach, which makes it easy to haul coolers, fishing gear, and camping supplies right to your spot. Four-wheel drive is recommended, especially after rain or in softer sand. Getting stuck is common for vehicles without the right tires or clearance, so if you are driving a standard car, park in the lot and walk to the beach instead.

If you prefer getting around without a car, golf cart rentals in Port Aransas are great for cruising around town, though golf carts are not allowed inside the state park.

Fees and Practical Details

Day-use entry is $7 per person for ages 13 and up. Children 12 and under get in free. If you have a Texas State Parks Pass, entry is included. The main day-use area has restrooms, outdoor showers, and picnic areas. The primitive camping zones do not have restroom facilities nearby.

Dogs are welcome in the park but must be on a leash no longer than six feet at all times. They are not allowed inside park buildings.

There are no shops, restaurants, or gas stations inside the park, so bring everything you need. Port Aransas is close enough for a supply run, but once you are settled on the beach, you will not want to leave.

Mustang Island State Park vs. Padre Island National Seashore

Visitors sometimes ask how Mustang Island State Park compares to Padre Island National Seashore, which is located about 45 minutes further south. Padre Island National Seashore is much larger (over 70 miles of coastline) and even more remote. It is managed by the National Park Service and has a stronger focus on conservation, including sea turtle protection programs.

Mustang Island State Park is more accessible, closer to town, and easier for a half-day or day trip. Padre Island is better suited for multi-day adventures where you want true isolation. Both are worth visiting if you have the time, but for most families staying in Port Aransas, Mustang Island State Park is the more practical choice.
