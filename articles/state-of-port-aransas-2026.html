The State of Port Aransas in 2026

What is new, what is changing, and what visitors should know this year

Home

Published on November 9, 2026

Port Aransas has always been one of those Texas beach towns that quietly does its thing while the bigger names get the attention. But 2026 has been a notable year for the island. A record whooping crane count, the 90th edition of the oldest fishing tournament on the Texas coast, and steady growth in the Cinnamon Shore community have all put Port Aransas in a strong position heading into the winter travel season.

Here is a look at where things stand.

Wildlife Is Having a Good Year

The headline number is the whooping crane population. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service estimated about 540 birds in the Aransas-Wood Buffalo population's primary survey area for the 2025-26 winter season, released in June 2026. That is a meaningful count for a species that was once down to fewer than 20 individuals in the wild. The cranes winter along the Texas coast near Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, which is a short drive from Port Aransas and one of the best places in the country to see them.

The refuge itself has recorded more than 400 bird species over the years, making it a destination for serious birders and casual wildlife watchers alike. The annual Whooping Crane Festival took place February 19 through 22 in Port Aransas, drawing visitors from across the country.

Kemp's ridley sea turtles continue nesting along Padre Island National Seashore, with the nesting season running from April through July. The National Park Service notes that more Kemp's ridleys nest at Padre Island than anywhere else in the United States. Hatchling releases, when they happen, are one of the more memorable wildlife experiences on the Texas coast, though they are not guaranteed on any given day.

Dolphins remain a regular sight along the Port Aransas ship channel, and dolphin watching tours continue to be one of the most popular activities for visitors.

Fishing Capital of Texas

In 2025, the Texas Legislature officially designated Port Aransas as the "Fishing Capital of Texas" through House Concurrent Resolution 117. The designation runs for ten years from the date of final passage, and it is not just ceremonial. Port Aransas has long been one of the top fishing destinations on the Gulf Coast, with deep-sea charters, bay fishing, pier fishing, and jetty fishing all accessible within a few minutes of town.

The Deep Sea Roundup, held July 9 through 12 in 2026, marked its 90th edition. It is the oldest fishing tournament on the Texas coast and one of the longest-running in the entire Gulf. The tournament draws anglers from across the state and beyond, and the week around it is one of the busiest on the island.

Beach Access and Conditions

The beaches on Mustang Island remain wide, accessible, and drive-on in designated areas. The city requires permits for beach parking in certain zones, with some free-parking exceptions. If you are driving on the beach, check the current permit requirements before you go.

Mustang Island State Park, located just south of Port Aransas, offers over five miles of coastline along with 20 miles of paddling trails in three segments on the bay side. The park is a strong option for visitors who want a quieter stretch of beach or want to try kayaking and paddleboarding in calmer water. More details are in the Mustang Island State Park guide.

Padre Island National Seashore, a separate park about 30 minutes south, stretches 70 miles along the coast. Most of it is accessible only by four-wheel-drive vehicle, which makes it one of the most remote and undeveloped stretches of coastline in the country. It is worth a day trip if you want to see what the Texas Gulf Coast looks like without any development at all.

Getting There in 2026

The free ferry from Aransas Pass continues to operate 24 hours a day, weather permitting. Each ferry carries up to 20 vehicles, with two to six ferries running at any given time depending on demand. The crossing itself takes under ten minutes, but wait times can stretch during peak weekends and holidays.

The alternative route through Corpus Christi via the JFK Causeway remains the more predictable option, especially on Friday afternoons when the ferry line backs up. From San Antonio, the drive is roughly three hours. From Austin, about three and a half. From Houston, roughly four hours.

Corpus Christi International Airport (CRP) is the closest airport to Port Aransas, about 35 to 45 minutes from the island. San Antonio and Austin airports are alternatives with more flight options for visitors coming from out of state.

Events Calendar

Here are the key events that shaped Port Aransas in 2026 and what is coming up.

February 19-22: Whooping Crane Festival in Port Aransas. Guided birding tours, boat trips to the refuge, and educational programs.

April through July: Kemp's ridley sea turtle nesting season on Padre Island National Seashore. Hatchling releases are announced on short notice when they happen.

July 9-12: Deep Sea Roundup, 90th edition. The oldest fishing tournament on the Texas coast.

April 16-18, 2027: Texas SandFest. The next confirmed edition of the annual sand sculpture competition. No 2026 dates were confirmed this year.

Winter 2026-27: Whooping cranes return to the Texas coast. The birds are typically present from November through March, with the best viewing at Aransas National Wildlife Refuge.

The Bottom Line

Port Aransas in 2026 is a town that continues to punch above its weight. A population of about 4,000 people supports a fishing tournament that has been running for 90 years, a wildlife refuge that draws birders from around the world, and a beach community that families keep returning to year after year. The official "Fishing Capital of Texas" designation is new, but the reputation behind it has been building for decades.

If you have been thinking about a trip to the Texas coast, this is a good year to go. The wildlife is thriving, the events are strong, and the island still has the laid-back feel that makes it different from every other beach town in the state.
